Preventing Frozen Pipes in an Empty Home

Why pipes burst in vacant homes, the thermostat and water settings that prevent it, when to winterize, which sensors help and what to do after a freeze.

By the Home.Watch editorial team. Updated Nov 18, 2026. 3 min read.

On this page

  1. Where pipes freeze first
  2. The four lines of defense
  3. During a cold snap
  4. If a pipe has frozen or burst

A burst pipe is the classic empty-house disaster. Water expands as it freezes, pressure builds between the ice and a closed faucet, and the pipe splits. Nothing happens until the ice thaws — then water pours out, often for days before anyone notices.

Where pipes freeze first

  • Exterior walls, especially behind kitchen and bathroom sinks
  • Crawl spaces, attics, garages and unheated basements
  • Hose bibs and outdoor faucets
  • Pipes near vents, windows or poorly sealed sill plates
  • Supply lines to ice makers and laundry rooms on outside walls

The four lines of defense

1. Keep heat on

Set the thermostat no lower than 55°F (13°C), and higher in very old or poorly insulated houses. Replace the thermostat battery before you leave. A remote-access thermostat lets you check that the furnace is holding temperature.

2. Shut off and drain the water

Turning off the main valve and opening faucets to drain the lines removes most of the water that could freeze. If the home will be empty all winter in a cold climate, ask a plumber to winterize it fully. Do not shut off water feeding a fire sprinkler system or a hot-water boiler without professional advice.

3. Protect the vulnerable spots

  • Insulate pipes in crawl spaces, attics and garages with foam sleeves
  • Seal gaps where pipes pass through exterior walls
  • Disconnect hoses and install insulated covers on outdoor faucets
  • Leave cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls slightly open so warm air circulates
  • Consider thermostatically controlled heat cable on problem runs

4. Monitor and respond

A low-temperature alert gives you hours to act instead of discovering a flood weeks later. Combine a temperature sensor, leak sensors at the water heater and sinks, and a power-loss alert — furnaces stop when the power goes out. Make sure someone local can respond: your home watcher, a neighbor or a plumber.

During a cold snap

  • Ask your home watcher for an extra visit
  • Confirm the furnace is running and the house is holding temperature
  • If the water is on, a slow drip at the faucet farthest from the main can relieve pressure

If a pipe has frozen or burst

  1. Shut off the main water valve immediately
  2. Turn off electricity to affected areas if water is near outlets or the panel
  3. Never use an open flame to thaw a pipe
  4. Photograph damage before cleanup for your insurer
  5. Call a plumber and a water-damage restoration company; drying should start within 24–48 hours to limit mold

Frequently asked questions

At what temperature do pipes freeze?

Pipes in unheated spaces become vulnerable when outdoor temperatures fall to around 20°F (−7°C) or below, especially for long periods, but exposed pipes can freeze at higher temperatures with wind chill. Keep indoor heat on and well above freezing.

Should I drain my pipes for the winter?

If the home will be unheated or empty for months in a cold climate, full winterization by a plumber — draining lines and adding non-toxic antifreeze to traps — is the safest option.
