Smart Home Monitoring for Second Homes: Sensors, Cameras and Connectivity

Build a practical remote-monitoring kit for a vacation or seasonal home: what to monitor, Wi-Fi versus cellular, power backup, privacy and who responds to alerts.

By the Home.Watch editorial team. Updated Nov 18, 2026. 3 min read.

On this page

  1. What to monitor, in order of value
  2. Connectivity: the weak link
  3. Smart thermostats
  4. Who responds?
  5. Privacy and rentals
  6. Test before every departure

Remote monitoring turns an empty house from a black box into something you can check from your phone. The goal is not gadgets for their own sake — it is getting told about the expensive failures early enough to act.

What to monitor, in order of value

  1. Water — leak sensors at every appliance and fixture, plus flow monitoring or an automatic shutoff if possible (leak detection guide)
  2. Temperature — low-temperature alerts for freeze risk, high-temperature alerts for AC failure
  3. Humidity — essential in hot, humid climates (humidity guide)
  4. Power — an outage stops furnaces, air conditioners, sump pumps and refrigerators
  5. Entry — door and window sensors, glass-break detectors and a monitored alarm
  6. Exterior cameras — package theft, trespassers, storm damage

Connectivity: the weak link

Most consumer devices rely on home Wi-Fi. In a seasonal home, internet may be disconnected to save money or may fail during the outage you most need to know about. Options:

  • Keep internet service active and put the router and hub on a battery backup
  • Choose devices that send alerts over cellular networks
  • Use a monitoring hub with cellular backup
  • Set up an alert if devices go offline — silence should be treated as a warning

Smart thermostats

A connected thermostat lets you confirm the house is holding temperature, adjust settings before you return and get alerts when it cannot keep up. Replace batteries before you leave and confirm the thermostat stays online.

Who responds?

Every alert needs a person who can be there within hours. Agree in advance with your home watcher on:

  • Which alerts you will forward to them
  • Their response time and fee for unscheduled visits
  • Whether they can access the device app directly

Privacy and rentals

If you rent your property, follow platform rules and local law on recording devices. Many platforms prohibit indoor cameras entirely. Disclose exterior cameras to guests.

Test before every departure

Trigger each sensor, confirm alerts arrive, check batteries and write down how to silence alarms. A sensor that silently died in month one is worse than none, because you think you are covered.

Frequently asked questions

What is the minimum monitoring for a second home?

Temperature, water leak and power-loss alerts cover the most expensive failures. Add a smart thermostat and a monitored alarm if budget allows.

Do I need cameras inside an empty home?

Not necessarily. Exterior cameras and door sensors cover most security needs. If you rent the home to guests, indoor cameras are often prohibited by rental platforms and local law.
