Protecting a Vacant Home From Break-ins and Squatters

How burglars and squatters spot empty homes, the lived-in signals that deter them, physical and electronic security, and what to do if someone moves in.

By the Home.Watch editorial team. Updated Nov 18, 2026. 3 min read.

On this page

  1. Signals that a home is empty
  2. Make it look lived-in
  3. Harden the entry points
  4. Electronic security
  5. The human factor
  6. If someone gets in

Empty homes send signals. Most intruders and squatters look for the easy target: a house that has obviously been empty for a while with nobody checking on it. Your job is to remove those signals and make entry slow and noisy.

Signals that a home is empty

  • Mail, flyers and packages piling up
  • An overgrown lawn or snow that has not been cleared
  • The same lights on or off for weeks
  • Closed blinds every day
  • No vehicle in the driveway for long periods
  • Social media posts announcing your trip

Make it look lived-in

  • Keep lawn care and snow removal on schedule
  • Put lights on timers with varied times, or use smart bulbs with an away mode
  • Ask a neighbor to park in the driveway occasionally
  • Stop mail or have it collected on every home watch visit
  • Post photos after you return, not while you are away

Harden the entry points

  • Deadbolts with a full one-inch throw and reinforced strike plates on exterior doors
  • Bars or locks for sliding doors
  • Secure the garage door and disconnect the emergency release from outside access
  • Lock gates, sheds and ladders that could be used to reach upper windows
  • Motion-activated exterior lights

Electronic security

  • A monitored alarm with door, window and glass-break sensors
  • Exterior cameras covering doors and driveways, with alerts to your phone
  • A sign and window decals announcing the alarm

The human factor

A home watcher who visits at unpredictable times, collects mail and photographs every door and window is one of the most effective deterrents available. Introduce them to neighbors so people know who should be at the house.

If someone gets in

  1. Do not enter or confront anyone
  2. Call local police
  3. Contact your insurer and document everything with photos
  4. If someone claims a right to stay, talk to a local attorney promptly; procedures differ between jurisdictions

Frequently asked questions

How do squatters find empty homes?

Common signs include overgrown yards, piled-up mail and packages, no lights or cars for weeks, and online listings for homes that are for sale or rent.

What should I do if someone is living in my vacant home?

Do not confront them yourself. Contact local police and an attorney. Squatter and trespass laws vary widely by state and province, and acting quickly matters.
