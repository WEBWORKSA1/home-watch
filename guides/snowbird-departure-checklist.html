The Snowbird Departure Checklist: Closing Your Home for the Season

A room-by-room checklist for closing a seasonal home: water, climate, power, security, insurance, mail and the final walk-through before you leave.

By the Home.Watch editorial team. Updated Nov 18, 2026. 3 min read.

On this page

  1. Two to four weeks before
  2. Water: the most important section
  3. Climate and power
  4. Security
  5. Outside
  6. Final walk-through on departure day

Closing a home properly takes a few hours and saves months of worry. Work through this list over the last week before you leave. Our Departure Checklist Generator builds a printable version tailored to your home and climate.

Two to four weeks before

  • Schedule your home watch visits and share your itinerary and emergency contacts
  • Call your insurance agent: confirm how your policy treats a vacant or unoccupied home and whether regular checks are required
  • Book any service you will need while away: pest control, lawn care, pool service
  • Arrange mail forwarding or a hold
  • Stop or pause newspaper and recurring deliveries
  • Service the HVAC system and replace filters
  • Check the batteries in smoke and carbon monoxide detectors

Water: the most important section

  • Shut off the main water supply unless a fire sprinkler system or boiler needs it
  • Turn off the water heater or set it to vacation mode
  • Open faucets briefly after shutting the main to relieve pressure
  • Turn off water to the ice maker and washing machine
  • Pour a little water into rarely used drains to keep traps sealed (your home watcher can top these up)
  • Set the irrigation system for the season, or turn it off
  • In freezing climates, consider having a plumber winterize the plumbing — see preventing frozen pipes

Climate and power

  • Set the thermostat for the season: no lower than 55°F in cold climates; 78–82°F with humidity control in hot, humid climates — see humidity and mold
  • Unplug small appliances, electronics and chargers
  • Leave the refrigerator running with a few items, or empty, clean and prop it open
  • Put a few lights on timers and vary the times
  • Test any leak, temperature and power-loss sensors and confirm they reach your phone

Security

  • Lock every door and window, including the garage and any pet doors
  • Remove the spare key from under the mat or planter
  • Set the alarm and give your home watcher the code and the monitoring company's number
  • Close blinds partially rather than fully so the house looks lived-in
  • Ask a neighbor to report anything unusual and introduce them to your home watcher
  • Pause package deliveries or route them to a locker

Outside

  • Clean gutters and downspouts
  • Secure or store outdoor furniture, grills, planters and decorations
  • Trim branches that touch the roof
  • In hurricane regions, plan who will put up shutters if a storm threatens — see our hurricane plan

Final walk-through on departure day

  • Take photos or a video of every room for insurance records
  • Empty all trash and remove food that can spoil or attract pests
  • Close interior doors to rooms you do not need ventilated, but leave cabinet doors under sinks slightly open in cold climates
  • Confirm the water main is off, the water heater is set and the thermostat is holding
  • Leave a printed contact sheet for your home watcher on the kitchen counter

Frequently asked questions

Should I turn off my main water valve when I leave?

In most cases, yes, unless a fire sprinkler system or a boiler depends on it. Shutting the main is one of the most effective ways to prevent a flood in an empty home. Confirm with a plumber if you have a sprinkler system or hydronic heat.

What temperature should I leave the thermostat at?

In cold climates many safety organizations recommend no lower than 55°F (13°C). In hot, humid climates owners commonly set the air conditioning between 78°F and 82°F to keep humidity under control.
