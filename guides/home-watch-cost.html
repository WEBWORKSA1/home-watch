How Much Does Home Watch Cost? Pricing, Visit Frequency and What to Expect

Typical home watch prices by visit frequency and home size, how providers structure fees, the add-ons that raise the bill and how to compare quotes fairly.

By the Home.Watch editorial team. Updated Nov 18, 2026. 3 min read.

On this page

  1. The four factors that set the price
  2. Typical ranges by frequency
  3. Common add-on charges
  4. How to compare quotes fairly
  5. Estimate your own cost
  6. Is it worth it?

Home watch pricing is simple once you know the moving parts. Most providers charge a flat monthly fee based on how often they visit and how much there is to check, then add one-time charges for extra services. This guide explains typical ranges and how to compare quotes without getting surprised later.

The figures below are illustrative ranges drawn from published provider price lists and industry norms. Prices vary by market and change over time. Always get written quotes.

The four factors that set the price

  1. Visit frequency. Weekly visits cost more per month than every-other-week visits, but often less per visit.
  2. Home size and complexity. A 4,500 sq ft home with a pool, two water heaters and an outdoor kitchen takes longer to inspect than a two-bedroom condo.
  3. Location and travel. Rural, island and gated properties can carry a distance or access surcharge.
  4. Service level. A ten-minute walk-through with a text message is a different product from a 45-minute documented inspection with photos.

Condos and small homes sit at the low end. Large homes, waterfront properties and luxury estates sit at or above the high end.

Common add-on charges

  • Storm preparation (shutters, outdoor furniture): often priced per event or per hour
  • Post-storm inspection: a flat fee per inspection
  • Vendor meetings and key holding: per visit or hourly
  • Pre-arrival setup: a flat fee, plus the cost of any groceries or supplies
  • Vehicle starts, pool checks, plant watering: small per-visit charges
  • Emergency call-outs outside scheduled visits: usually hourly

How to compare quotes fairly

Put every quote on the same basis before choosing. Ask each provider:

  • How long does a typical visit take for my home?
  • Exactly what is on the checklist, inside and out?
  • How and when is the report delivered, and does it include photos?
  • What is included in the monthly fee and what is extra?
  • Are you insured, bonded and background-checked? Can I see certificates?
  • What is your response time if a sensor alerts me between visits?
  • Is there a contract term or cancellation fee?

A cheaper quote with a shorter checklist can cost far more if a leak is missed. The real comparison is price per thorough visit, not price per month.

Is it worth it?

Compare the seasonal cost to the deductible on your homeowners policy and the price of a single water-damage claim. For many owners, one caught leak or one avoided mold remediation covers years of visits. The service can also help you stay within the vacancy conditions of your insurance policy — see our vacancy clause guide.

Frequently asked questions

What is a typical monthly price for weekly home watch?

For an average-size single-family home, weekly visits commonly fall somewhere between about $100 and $250 per month, depending on the market, home size and how detailed each visit is. Get local quotes to confirm.

Why do quotes vary so much?

Providers differ in visit length, checklist depth, report quality, insurance and bonding, travel distance and whether storm checks or vendor meetings are included.

Is home watch tax deductible?

Generally not for a personal residence. It may be a deductible expense for a rental property. Ask a tax professional about your situation.
